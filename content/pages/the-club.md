# The club

A friendly non-profit club, founded in 1986, flying from Birch Hills Airport. We are affiliated with the Soaring Association of Saskatchewan and the Soaring Association of Canada.

Gliding is a team sport. It takes a crew to launch a glider, and members keep the cost of flying low by pitching in: driving the winch, running a wing, looking after the gliders and the field.

## The fleet

Three gliders: a two-seat trainer and two single-seaters.

### Schleicher K7 Rhönadler · Two-seat trainer

Where every first flight and lesson happens. Steel-tube fuselage with wood and fabric wings, designed by Rudolf Kaiser as a trainer.

| Seats | Wingspan | Best glide | Min. sink | Empty weight | Introduced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 16.0 m | 26:1 | 0.70 m/s | 282 kg | 1960 |

### Schleicher Ka 6E · Single-seater

One of the most loved and best-handling gliders ever made. A classic wooden sailplane, also a Rudolf Kaiser design, with an all-flying tailplane.

| Seats | Wingspan | Best glide | Min. sink | Empty weight | Introduced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 15.0 m | 32:1 | 0.65 m/s | 190 kg | 1965 |

### Bölkow Phoebus C · Single-seater

An early fibreglass sailplane with a 17-metre wing, a T-tail and a retractable wheel. The highest performer of the three.

| Seats | Wingspan | Best glide | Min. sink | Empty weight | Introduced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 17.0 m | 39:1 | 0.63 m/s | 240 kg | 1967 |

### Reading the numbers

A best glide of 26:1 means 26 metres forward for every metre of height lost: about 16 km from a 2,000 ft launch in still air. Minimum sink is how slowly the glider descends when flown for time aloft.
