# Strategy

## The one job

Get a visitor to request a first flight. Everything else on the site either helps that happen or helps a first-timer come back and join.

- **Primary conversion:** a Discovery Flight request (form submitted).
- **Secondary:** a paid Discovery Flight or gift flight (Wave payment link), a membership form opened, a training inquiry.

The Discovery Flight is presented as a first lesson, not a ride. The club has one two-seat trainer and volunteer instructors, so seat time is scarce; the framing attracts people who may go on to join. Whether the club's priority is members or ride revenue is an open question (`06-open-questions.md`, Q1). Until it is answered, build for members.

## The funnel

| Stage | What the visitor does | What the site does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find | Searches, follows a link, is given a gift | Local-search pages, clear titles, a real photo and the price on first screen |
| Request | Fills in the short form | Asks only what is needed; says who replies and how soon |
| Confirm | Hears back from a person | A club member confirms a date and adds them to the schedule |
| Fly | Comes to Birch Hills | Confirmation email carries the welcome pack: what to bring, when to arrive, how to check the day is on |
| Join | Comes back | Follow-up message after the flight; Learn to fly page makes the next step and its cost plain |

Guests never see the Google Sheet. The sheet stays the club's operating tool; members use it directly, from the Members page.

A request is a request, not an instant booking. Weather and volunteer availability mean a person has to confirm. The site says so in the three steps: request, we confirm, come and fly.

## Audiences

Each gets its own page with its own hook and the same button.

| Who | What they want to know | Hook | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First-timers and gift buyers | Cost, safety, what it is like, how to book, can I give it | $50, about 2,000 feet, flown with an instructor | Discovery Flight |
| Adults who want to learn | Total cost, how long, am I too old, how it fits a life | Free instruction, about $10 a lesson flight | Learn to fly |
| Youth, Air Cadets and their parents | Cost, supervision, age, cadet wings | $15 membership, $1,000 in bursaries, solo at 14 | Youth and cadets |
| Power pilots | What is waived, what counts | Licence in 3 flight hours, no ground school or exam | Power pilots |

## Principles

1. **One primary action per page.** The button is in the header on every page and repeated at the foot.
2. **Price early.** The club's prices are its strongest argument. They are on the first screen of the home and Discovery Flight pages.
3. **Answer the worry before it is asked.** Weather cancellation, who is in command, weight and age limits, what to bring.
4. **Show the club is alive.** Real, recent photos; the next flying days pulled from the schedule; dated stories.
5. **Phone first.** Most first visits will be on a phone.
6. **Small and fast.** A handful of pages, no sliders, no pop-ups, no chat widget.
7. **Do not promise what volunteers cannot keep.** No live availability, no guaranteed dates.

## Voice

- Speak to the visitor: "you", "your first flight". The club is "we".
- Plain and concrete. Numbers with units. Short sentences.
- Lead with what the visitor can do, then what it costs, then the detail.
- Distances as drive times: 25 minutes from Prince Albert, 1.5 hours from Saskatoon.
- Club achievements are "out of PAGSC", never "by our members".
- Lessons in the air are "flight lessons". Ground school is separate and self-directed, or taken with pilottraining.ca.
- Compare gliding with powered flying the way sailing compares with motorboating. Never run powered flying down; the site also recruits power pilots.
- The idea to carry: gliding is a sport you never finish. Do not use the word "evergreen".

Do not write:

- Anything about the page itself, how it was made, or what is simplified.
- Lists of things the visitor does not need to do.
- "Most people do" or any claim about how many guests join.
- "Don't assume" or other phrasing that scolds.
- Superlatives the club cannot back ("cheapest in Saskatchewan").
- Invented statistics, ratings, quotes or testimonials.

## Follow-up

Kept deliberately small for a volunteer club:

1. **Confirmation** after a request: what happens next and when, plus the welcome pack.
2. **After the flight:** a short note with a link to Learn to fly and the membership form, and a request for a Google review.

No abandoned-form nudges, no retargeting, no newsletter at launch.

## Being found

- A Google Business Profile for the club at Birch Hills Airport, with current photos and the same phone, email and price as the site.
- Page titles and headings that match real searches: "glider flight Prince Albert", "gliding Saskatoon", "learn to fly gliders Saskatchewan", "glider flight gift".
- Structured data for the organisation and its location.
- The same price, contact details and location wording everywhere the club is listed.

## Measuring

One number matters: Discovery Flight requests. Then, by hand at season's end: requests, flights flown, new members.

Use a privacy-light analytics tool with no cookie banner (choice in `06-open-questions.md`, Q10). Track page views and one event, `flight_request_submitted`, with the page it came from.
